Common questions about ETW.

What is Explaining The World?+

Explaining The World is an independent project producing long-form researched content on history, focused on depth and accuracy. It is built for people who want more than a surface overview — who want to understand not just what happened, but why, and in the words of the sources themselves. Work is in progress on additional subjects and languages.

Who is behind ETW?+

ETW is an independent project with no corporate ownership, no investors, and no editorial board. There is one standard: quality. Details about individuals involved are not published publicly.

What topics does ETW cover?+

Currently: history — empires, leaders, wars, and the mechanics of power across civilisations. The scope will expand to other subjects as the project matures. Nothing is added until it can be done to the same standard as the existing work.

How is ETW content researched?+

Each topic begins with source identification across multiple references. Claims are cross-checked before anything is accepted as reliable. Important details are extracted, compiled into working notes, and verified again before being used in a final script or article. We do not rely on a single source for any significant claim. When sources disagree, we say so. When something cannot be verified, we do not state it as fact.

How often does ETW publish?+

There is no fixed schedule. A focused piece on a single event or text takes less time than a broad historical analysis spanning decades or centuries. Each piece takes as long as the research demands. We do not publish before a topic is ready.

Does ETW have a bias?+

We make every effort to present history as the sources record it — without favouring any nation, figure, religion, ideology, or political position. We acknowledge that no analysis is entirely neutral, but we actively work against bias at every stage of research and writing. If you identify something that reads as biased, we welcome the feedback through our contact form.

Is ETW content accurate?+

We hold accuracy as our primary standard. Mistakes can and do occur in any research process — when identified, we correct them fully and without reservation. We do not quietly edit errors; corrections are acknowledged. ETW content is educational and reflects our reading of available sources, not a final or officially endorsed historical record.

Can I use ETW content in my own work?+

Short quotations with clear credit and a source link are generally acceptable for commentary, criticism, or education. Republishing, reuploading, or redistributing ETW content without prior written permission is not permitted. For specific permissions, contact us. Full details are on the Legal page.

Can I suggest a topic?+

Yes. Topic suggestions can be sent through the contact form. We cannot guarantee a response to every suggestion or commit to covering any specific topic, but we read all messages and topic suggestions inform our planning.
